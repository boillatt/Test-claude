# University Innovation, Venture & Entrepreneurship Offices Across 19 European/Israeli Markets

## TL;DR

- Verified innovation/venture/entrepreneurship office URLs are provided for institutions across all 19 countries; coverage is strongest for Switzerland, Germany, Israel and Spain (roughly 8–20 verified each) and thinner for several smaller markets where a distinct, findable office URL was confirmed for only a handful of institutions.
- Where the closest available equivalent is a technology-transfer office (TTO) rather than an entrepreneurship/venture office, it is explicitly labeled as such (notably several Israeli institutions, University of Bergen, and Charles University in Czechia).
- Entries without a confirmed, verifiable URL were deliberately omitted rather than guessed, so several countries list fewer than the 20-institution ceiling.

## Key Findings

- The most mature university venture ecosystems are concentrated in Munich, Stockholm, Copenhagen/Lyngby, Delft, Leuven and Espoo. Munich's UnternehmerTUM — founded in 2002 by Susanne Klatten and repeatedly ranked #1 in the Financial Times/Statista "Europe's Leading Start-up Hubs" list — reports supporting more than 1,000 startups since 2002  and over 5,000 participants in its programs each year,  and TUM itself reports more than 70 technology-oriented company foundations annually,  with TUM Venture Labs spanning multiple technology fields.
- Stockholm's KTH Innovation reports meeting over 400 teams with new ideas each year  and helping start more than 450 companies that have together raised over 8 billion Swedish kronor  since its 2007 launch, with a strong deep-tech and sustainability orientation.
- Germany has an unusually dense national network of university start-up centers, many supported through the federal EXIST program (run by the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs); UnternehmerTUM is now the explicit template for a federal "Startup Factories" initiative intended to replicate the model at additional German universities.
- Israel's model is distinctive: several leading institutions operate through commercialization companies (Yeda, Ramot, BIRAD, T3, Carmel) alongside newer dedicated entrepreneurship centers (Yazamut 360° at Ben-Gurion University, ASPER-HUJI Innovate at Hebrew University, and Reichman University's Adelson School of Entrepreneurship).

## Recommendations

- **Stage 1 — Adopt the verified core.** The tables above are ready to use; all listed URLs were surfaced from official institutional domains during research or confirmed by a dedicated verification pass. Treat labeled TTO entries as the "closest equivalent" where no distinct venture/entrepreneurship office was confirmable.
- **Stage 2 — Fill the thin countries first.** The highest-value extensions are in markets currently below the 20-institution ceiling. Priority targets to verify next (each has a well-known office but its URL was not confirmed within budget): UK — Oxford University Innovation, Imperial Enterprise Lab, UCL Innovation & Enterprise, University of Edinburgh Bayes Centre/Edinburgh Innovations, University of Manchester Innovation Factory; Portugal — UPTEC (U. Porto), Instituto Pedro Nunes (Coimbra), NOVA/Madan Parque (Lisbon); Italy — I3P (Politecnico di Torino), Almacube (U. Bologna), LUISS EnLabs; Belgium — UGent/DO! and Ghent tech transfer, UCLouvain (Sopartec); Ireland — NovaUCD, DCU Invent; Netherlands — YES!Delft, UtrechtInc, Erasmus Centre for Entrepreneurship; Poland — Warsaw University of Technology and AGH tech-transfer/incubators; Greece — Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, Athens University of Economics & Business; Czechia — CTU Prague (InovaJET/eClub), Brno University of Technology.
- **Stage 3 — Rank and cap at 20.** When extending each country, order candidates by research intensity and demonstrated venture output (spin-off counts, capital raised) rather than padding to hit 20.
- **Benchmark that would change the approach:** if a target institution's office URL cannot be confirmed on the institution's own domain after one focused search + fetch, exclude it (consistent with the no-guess rule) rather than substituting an aggregator or news link.
- **Maintenance:** re-verify URLs roughly quarterly — universities frequently restructure innovation units and change URL paths (several German and Spanish offices have rebranded in the last two years).

## Caveats

- **TTO substitutions are labeled.** Several entries (most Israeli institutions, University of Bergen/VIS, Charles University/CPPT) are technology-transfer or commercialization bodies used as the closest available equivalent; they are marked in the "Note" column. Where both an entrepreneurship center and a TTO exist (e.g., Hebrew University's ASPER-HUJI Innovate vs. Yissum; Ben-Gurion's Yazamut 360° vs. BGN Technologies), the entrepreneurship-framed office was chosen per the task's priority.
- **A small number of Swedish entries** (Chalmers Ventures, SSE Business Lab, Uppsala Innovation Centre) rely on domains named consistently across multiple secondary sources rather than a directly fetched institutional page; these should be re-confirmed before publication.
- **Coverage is uneven by design.** Countries such as Portugal, Poland and Greece show only one or two entries because verifiable, institution-owned office URLs could not be confirmed within the research budget — not because additional such offices do not exist. The strongest, most confidently verified national lists are Switzerland, Germany, Israel and Spain.
- **Some URLs point to a hub/landing page** that aggregates a university's innovation and entrepreneurship activity (e.g., TU Delft, PSL, Aalto, Trinity) rather than a single named "office," reflecting how those universities organize the function.
- **Duplicate institutional rows** (e.g., two TU Delft, two PSL, two Charles University entries) are included intentionally where a university runs distinct but complementary units (an entrepreneurship/education arm plus a venture/commercialization arm); de-duplicate to one row per institution if a strict one-per-university list is required.
